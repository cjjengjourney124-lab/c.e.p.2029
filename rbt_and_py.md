Here is exactly where to go and the specific courses you should look for, along with a few excellent YouTube channels that focus on this.

1. The Official Onshape Learning Center (Best Starting Point)

Since you already have an account, log in and go to onshape.com. You want to search for and complete these three short, free courses in order:
• Course 1: "Part Design Using Part Studios"
	• Why: This teaches you how to design multiple distinct parts (like the base, the arm, and the claw) inside a single document so they line up perfectly.
• Course 2: "Introduction to Assembly Design"
	• Why: This is the exact course that teaches you "Mates." You will learn how to use a Revolute Mate (which allows a joint to spin like a wheel) and a Slider Mate (which allows a joint to slide back and forth).
• Course 3: "Document Management"
	• Why: This will teach you how to create a clean, public link to your 3D model so you can share it with colleges or put it on a resume without downloading anything.

2. YouTube Channels for Visual, Step-by-Step Builds

If you prefer watching someone build a project from scratch while you follow along on a second tab, search YouTube for these channels:
• "Onshape" (Official Channel): Search their channel for the playlist titled "Onshape Assemblies." They have short 2-to-3 minute videos showing exactly how to connect different moving parts.
• "Milton STEM": This channel is run by a high school STEM teacher who creates incredible, student-friendly Onshape tutorials. Search his channel for "Onshape Robotic Arm" or "Onshape Assembly Basics"—his steps are incredibly easy to follow.

🤖 Your 4-Step Build Blueprint

When you are ready to start drawing the arm in Onshape, use this simple blueprint to keep it manageable:
1. The Base: Sketch a simple, flat square block. This will stay locked in place.
2. The Lower Arm: Sketch a long rectangle with rounded edges. Draw a circle at the bottom where it connects to the base, and a circle at the top where it connects to the next piece.
3. The Upper Arm: Make another similar link that will connect to the lower arm.
4. The Gripper/Claw: Sketch a simple "V" shape or a hook that will represent your robot's hand.
Once you have those 4 parts modeled, open the Assembly tab, insert all 4 parts, and use Revolute Mates to connect the circles. You will instantly have a moving robotic mechanism!

1. The Big Project Idea: A 3D-Modeled Virtual Robotic Arm

Since you are in PLTW Principles of Engineering, you can bridge your 3D modeling and your coding together by designing a robotic mechanism in Onshape and scripting its logic in Replit.
• Step 1: The Onshape Assembly (Multi-Part)
	• Instead of making one solid object, create a Document with multiple Part Studios.
	• Model a base, a lower arm segment, an upper arm segment, and a simple two-finger claw as separate parts.
	• Move over to the Assembly tab in Onshape. Use Revolute Mates and Slider Mates to connect the pieces. This will allow you to drag the digital arm on your screen and watch the joints rotate and move realistically!
• Step 2: The Replit Controller (The Coding Side)
	• In Replit, write a Python program that simulates the control panel for your robotic arm.
	• You can code user inputs (e.g., typing "U" to move the arm up, "O" to open the claw) and use math logic to track the (x, y) coordinates of where the robot claw is moving.
